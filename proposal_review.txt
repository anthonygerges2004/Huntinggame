2. Project Abstract
Brief Description
This Unity first-person wildlife hunting game lets the player hunt, loot meat, chop wood, and trade. Birds scatter after a shot, meat spoils unless cooked, and one legendary animal is rare. Fire, night attacks, and a camp trader complete the wildlife loop.
Word count: 42 words.
3. Introduction
Project Overview
The project is a first-person hunting game set in a wildlife area, not a score-only shooting gallery. The player lives off the land: hunt animals, take meat and other materials from kills, gather wood, keep a fire, stay alive, and trade extra goods for better gear. The world has a morning and a night. Day is safer for hunting and gathering. Night is dangerous: animals can attack if the player has no fire.
Wildlife is mixed. Birds are the most common targets and give a small amount of meat. Medium animals (for example deer) appear less often and give more loot. Large animals are rare but can be found; they drop more meat and valuable items, and they are a bigger threat. After a kill, the animal is not only removed for points: the player loots meat and related materials into an inventory.
The player can also harvest wood from trees, but only while carrying a pickaxe. Wood is used to make a fire at any time and can be sold. A trade post (a camp or trader) is a fixed place on the map where the player exchanges meat, wood, and weapons. The player has a limited life (health). Reaching zero health ends the hunt. A campfire nearby at night stops animal attacks; without fire, night wildlife can damage the player.
What makes the project special is that the forest reacts. A gunshot or a kill makes birds fly up and nearby animals run. Meat is not a permanent score: it spoils unless the player cooks it on the fire or sells it in time. Cooking restores health, so the fire matters in the day as well as at night. One legendary animal appears rarely (for example at dawn, after a rumor at the trader). Extra systems include short-lived tracks, overhunting that empties an area, a trader who is also a rival hunter, and quiet crouch movement so animals hear running and shooting more easily.
A Unity 6 prototype already exists: first-person move and look, raycast shooting, placeholder deer, and a score HUD. The problem is to grow that prototype into a complete wildlife loop (hunt ΓåÆ loot ΓåÆ cook or trade ΓåÆ gather ΓåÆ fire ΓåÆ survive day and night) plus the special systems above. The proposed solution keeps the current player and raycast weapon and adds inventory, rarity, day/night, campfire, trader, trees, health, and the unique hunting rules (alarm, spoilage, legendary animal).
4. Motivation and Scope
Motivation
We want a hunting game that also feels like living in the wild. Shooting an animal should matter because it gives meat, not only a number on the screen. Chopping wood, lighting a fire, and waiting for morning create tension that a timed score hunt does not. Trading gives a reason to store extra meat and wood instead of ending the session after a few shots. Special rules make the wildlife feel alive: animals warn each other, meat spoils, and a legendary animal is the hunt everyone talks about.
The idea started from the Unity prototype (player, camera, shoot, score) and from survival games where night, fire, and gathering change how the player moves.
Scope
In scope (minimum viable product):
First-person movement, mouse look, and hitscan shooting.
Wildlife types: common birds, less common medium animals, rare large animals.
Loot from kills: meat and simple materials go into an inventory.
Chop wood from trees only if the player has a pickaxe.
Place or light a campfire at any time (using wood).
Day/night (morning and night) with different lighting.
Player health (life limit); game over at zero health.
At night, animals can attack the player if no fire is active nearby.
One trade place to buy/sell weapons, meat, and wood.
A HUD for health, time of day, meat freshness, and inventory counts.
GitHub repository with a Unity .gitignore.
Special features (what makes this hunt unique):
Animals warn each other: a shot or kill makes birds scatter and nearby deer flee.
Meat spoils after a short time unless it is cooked on the fire or sold; cooking restores health.
One legendary animal is very rare (rumor at the trader; may appear at dawn).
Large animals leave tracks for a short time; the player can follow them instead of a map marker.
Overhunting: killing too many animals in one area makes that zone quieter and night more dangerous.
The trader is a rival hunter who also wants the legendary animal if the player is too slow.
Crouch / quiet movement: running and shooting alert wildlife more than slow movement.
Hunt challenges: optional goals on the HUD, such as killing a bird while standing under a tree, hitting an animal from a set distance (20 meters or more), or killing a deer while crouched.
Assumptions:
Single-player, keyboard and mouse, Unity editor or Windows build.
Placeholder models (capsules, simple trees) are allowed until better assets are added.
One compact map with one trader camp, not an open world.
ΓÇ£PickaxeΓÇ¥ is the required tool for chopping trees in this game (gameplay rule, not realism).
Out of scope / boundaries:
Graphic gore, realistic butchering, or blood systems.
Multiplayer, crafting trees beyond fire, farming, or vehicles.
Online trading, accounts, mobile ports, or huge streaming maps.
Full hunting-license simulation or realistic ballistics.
5. Related Work
Similar Systems
Hunting games such as theHunter: Call of the Wild focus on tracking wildlife and taking a shot. Survival games such as The Forest, Green Hell, and Valheim add gathering, fire, night danger, and a camp. Our project sits between those two: hunting is the main activity, but meat, wood, fire, health, and a trader make the wildlife area a place to live, not only a shooting range.
Unity FPS samples show move, look, and shoot. We reuse that idea for the hunter, then add loot, inventory, and a day/night threat that a normal FPS does not have. Compared with Minecraft-style survival, the focus stays on hunting animals of different rarity (birds vs. rare large animals) and on a single trade post rather than a full crafting economy. Special hunting rules (alarming birds, spoiling meat, a legendary target, tracks, and a rival trader) separate this project from a generic FPS or a simple score hunt.
6. System Architecture
Basic Architecture
The game is one Unity scene: a wildlife map, trees, a trader camp, and a player. The Player object keeps the Character Controller, camera, PlayerMove, and PlayerShoot from the prototype, and adds PlayerHealth and Inventory. Animals are typed (Bird, Medium, RareLarge) with spawn weights, loot tables, and a night attack behaviour when no campfire is active. DayNightCycle drives lighting. Campfire is a placeable object that consumes wood and creates a safe radius at night. Trees require a pickaxe in inventory before they give wood. The Trader is a trigger zone with a simple buy/sell UI and can act as a rival for the legendary animal. Special scripts handle bird scatter, meat timers, cooking, tracks, overhunting, and crouch detection.
Component Interaction
Core Gameplay Flow
Example data flow: PlayerShoot hits a deer ΓåÆ nearby birds scatter and deer flee ΓåÆ Animal.Die() ΓåÆ Inventory.Add(meat with a spoil timer). If meat is cooked on the campfire, health is restored; if not, it becomes worthless. A legendary spawn is rare and may be claimed by the rival trader if the player waits too long. At night, DayNightCycle sets night. If Campfire.IsActive is false, NightThreat damages PlayerHealth. Chopping a tree fails unless Inventory.Has(pickaxe).
7. Goals and Objectives
Final Outcome
The expected result is a playable first-person wildlife hunting prototype. A classmate should be able to hunt, loot meat, cook or sell it before it spoils, follow tracks to a legendary animal, cut wood with a pickaxe, light a fire, survive a night, and trade at camp. Planned functionalities:
Control a first-person hunter (walk, look, shoot, lock/unlock cursor).
Find birds often; find large animals rarely.
Collect meat and related items from killed animals.
Collect wood from trees only with a pickaxe.
Light a fire at any time using wood.
See morning and night; night animals attack if there is no fire.
Have a health/life limit; dying ends the run.
Trade weapons, meat, and wood at one camp.
Show health, day/night, meat freshness, and inventory on the HUD.
Keep the project on GitHub for backup and instructor review.
Special features
These systems are what make the project more than a generic shooter. They are part of the planned outcome:
Animals warn each other: birds scatter and nearby animals flee after a shot or a kill.
Meat spoils unless cooked on the fire or sold in time; cooked meat restores health.
One legendary animal is very rare and is the main ΓÇ£wowΓÇ¥ hunt of the demo.
Tracks: large/legendary animals leave footprints that fade after a short time.
Overhunting: too many kills in one area reduces spawns and makes night worse.
Rival trader: the camp trader also hunts the legendary animal if the player delays.
Stealth: crouch to stay quieter; running and shooting alert wildlife.
Hunt challenges: short optional goals besides free hunting. A shade-bird challenge requires killing a bird while standing under a tree. A long-shot challenge requires hitting an animal from 20 meters or more. A quiet-deer challenge requires killing a deer while crouched. Progress is shown on the HUD.
Success is a stable Play-mode demo of the full loop (hunt, loot, cook or sell, fire, night, trade) plus at least the three core special features (scatter, spoiling meat, legendary animal), even if models are placeholders.
